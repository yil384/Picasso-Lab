## Stack and pipeline
- p5.js 2.x (WEBGL canvas) + p5.brush 2.x for all painting; a 2D compositor canvas for lettering/karaoke; Permanent Marker + Shantell Sans fonts.
- `studio.html` loads core.js, clawd.js, cast.js, props.js, lyrics.js, timeline.js and nine chapter files `src/ch/c01..c09` (~7,000 lines of chapter code, 500-900 lines each).
- `render.mjs` (puppeteer-core + system Chrome): `--sheet=t1,t2,...` renders a contact sheet with ms/frame labels (the agents' visual check), `--stills`, `--clip` (pipes JPEGs into ffmpeg with the song), `--frames` (parallel, resumable, atomic JPEG writes, N worker pages), `--encode` (libx264 crf 17 + aac).
- Every shot is a pure function `fn(t, lt, dur)` of song time; no Math.random, `hash(i)` for stable randomness, `jit()` reseeded 12x/s so outlines "boil" like hand-drawn animation. This is what makes parallel out-of-order rendering and parallel subagent authorship work.
- Rendered here on an M2 (Metal ANGLE; had to switch `--use-angle=d3d11` to `metal`): 80-950 ms per 1920x1080 frame.

## How Claude was guided
- Two generations. Gen 1 (`legacy/flash-version.html`, a single canvas-2D page with a play button: reads like an animated web page). Gen 2 was a rewrite after a human instruction to use p5.brush and make every scene transition into the next.
- The main thread wrote STORYBOARD.md (shot table: Time | Lyric | Shot | Out-transition, a palette per chapter, a cast table, "Rule for every shot: something happens", "Keep it text-light", "Motivated transitions", "Every shot has a push, pan, tilt, whip, zoom-out or on-beat shake", "Bigger dancers: lead Clawd ~40% of frame height").
- ANIMATION_GUIDE.md is the subagent brief: chapter registration API, purity rules, "only edit your own chapter file, report shared bugs, don't edit them", painting API table, character rig API (poses, 15 eye types, hats, lunchbox mouth, emotes), `mood()` for blink-squash-pop face changes (never snap), `move()` beat-synced dance styles, the stage props, style rules, a per-frame cost budget (<= 2.5 s), and a self-check loop: render a contact sheet, open it with the Read tool, check first/last frame of every shot, motion every 0.1 s around hits, transitions, karaoke-safe band.
- Parallelism: one chapter file per subagent, shared files frozen.

## Look (frames I rendered and viewed)
- Picture-book watercolour: paper texture under every frame, multiplied grain + vignette, soft bleeding washes for skies and backdrops (the sunburst stage backdrop is a genuinely nice wet-on-wet bloom), flat-washed characters with tapered, slightly boiling ink outlines.
- Clawd is a terracotta rectangle with stubby legs and simple kaomoji faces; the Researcher is a small chibi in a lab coat. Strong silhouettes, very readable at thumbnail size.
- Every lyric is a staged gag: loss-curve sled ride, villain throne chair with crown and coffee-mug tower, Chinese-room box in the sky, shinigami red eyes with ink speed lines, treadmill + black hole with "VWOOMP", parallax hills, two-galaxy-fan GPU in space, hard-hat Clawds at a vault, neural-net dance class, go-kart "SKRRT!", sleeping security-guard clouds with "HONK!", paperclip surf wave, paperclip planet with a bomb fuse, jazz club with orthogonal spotlights and a sax-playing Clawd, Clawd totem with attention arcs, data-centre aisle in one-point perspective, a loom, recursive scaffolding, "SLAM!" door with chains, theatre curtain call with all guest monsters.
- Transitions are motivated: e.g. 22.0-23.8 s: hallway door, Clawd's lunchbox mouth opens, jaws close over the camera to black with "CHOMP!", then a tooth-edged mouth iris opens onto the stage and the camera keeps pushing.
- Weak spots: geometry is simple polygons (rectangles, ellipses), so many props look clip-art flat; some backgrounds are a flat wash with a few shapes; perspective rooms (Chinese room, vault) are stiff; the karaoke bar is always on screen; texture is p5.brush procedural, not real paper scans. Lettering (Permanent Marker) is good for SFX.

## Quality
8/10 for charm and comic timing; craft is "very good web-native watercolour", not hand-animated. The best open example of the 2D pattern.

## What Picasso Lab should borrow
- The two-document method: a STORYBOARD (Time | line | shot | Out-transition, palette arc, text-light rule) and an ANIMATION_GUIDE that is a real API brief for parallel subagents.
- Pure `f(t)` shots + hash randomness + 12 fps boil reseed; contact-sheet render + Read-tool review loop with explicit checklist.
- A small reusable character rig with `mood()` (blink, squash take, emote pop) and `move()` beat-synced dances; recurring set (the stage) that escalates.
- Motivated transitions per shot (iris through a mouth/eye, fall, rocket, flash) and a camera move in every shot.
- p5.brush watercolour fills for backgrounds and glows, flat washes for characters.
