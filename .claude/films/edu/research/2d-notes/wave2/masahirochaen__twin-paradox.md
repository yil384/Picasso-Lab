## What I saw (structure: hard cuts between ~8 scenes, no continuous camera)
- 0-3 s: night city of torn-paper buildings with lit windows; a girl and a boy with a telescope in a window; a smiling
  paper sun with petal rays; paper planes. Cut.
- 3-8 s: a notepad page on cork; a paper hand with a pen WRITES "時間って、みんな同じ速さで進むの？" (does time pass at the
  same speed for everyone?) stroke by stroke. Cut to the sun holding the question card, "!" pops.
- 10-11 s: sunburst rays: "じつは…ちがうんだ。" (actually... it doesn't) on a pinned paper card.
- 12-20 s: the light clock on graph paper: label tapes "光の時計", "光の速さはだれから見ても同じ"; top row a resting clock
  (a light ball bouncing between two mirrors), bottom row a moving clock whose ball traces a dotted zig-zag; tick
  counters 5回 vs 3回 and analogue dials; caption strip "だから、時計がゆっくり進む！". The two rows run in sync so the
  difference in counts is visible. The best explanatory beat.
- 20-23 s: the twin in a paper rocket on a paper Earth, "光の速さの99%で 宇宙旅行へ！".
- 24-31 s: split screen: left navy space with the rocket (宇宙船 光速の99%), right a yellow room (地球) where calendar pages
  fly off (2026 -> 2039 -> 2092 -> 2097) and the brother ages into a grandpa; counters 0年->10年 vs 0年->71年;
  "宇宙船の10年 = 地球の約71年".
- 32-35 s: reunion on a green hill: "ただいま！" / "……おかえり" speech bubbles; caption "これが「ふたごのパラドックス」".
- 36-39 s: "本当の話": Earth with orbiting GPS satellites and a phone map: "GPS衛星の時計も相対性理論で補正されている",
  "補正しないと…".
- 40-46 s: back to the opening window, now girl + grandpa; "速く動くほど、時間はゆっくり流れる" pinned card; credit
  "特殊相対性理論（アインシュタイン・1905年）". Ending rhymes with the opening.
- Material at full res: torn white deckle edges on every paper piece, paper-fibre mottling, soft drop shadows, stitched
  dashes on the sky, round chibi faces with blush; CJK in a rounded, hand-drawn-looking face on torn paper tapes and
  pinned cards. It reads as craft paper, not as vector UI.

## Quality (candid): 7.5/10
The closest analogue to our Chinese-cut needs: a real physics idea, a hook question, one honest mechanism (the light
clock), numbers that land (10 vs 71 years), a real-world payoff (GPS), and a rhyme ending, in 46 s, with CJK lettering
that looks crafted. Weak spots: it is a sequence of hard-cut scenes (slideshow structure saved by material), the light
clock is still "a diagram on graph paper with label tapes", the 99% c / 10 vs 71 years numbers are stated rather than
derived, faces barely act, and it is square, not vertical.

## Borrow
- Cut-paper material for CJK lettering: captions as torn paper tapes and pinned cards that are part of the world.
- The explainer spine: question written by a hand on screen -> "actually, no" -> one mechanism shown side by side in sync
  -> a split-screen counter race -> a human payoff -> real-world use -> rhyme with the opening.
- Split screen works natively in 9:16 if stacked top/bottom (their light-clock rows already are).
## Avoid
- Hard-cutting between scenes with nothing carried; label tapes as the main explanation; the letterboxed repack of a
  square film for the vertical platforms.
