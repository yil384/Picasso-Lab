**Winner: B, by a narrow margin.** It needs A's grafts (section 6) and the fixes in section 7.

Why B wins on a near tie:
1. **Slideshow risk is the bible's top risk** (SERIES section 0 item 8; `research/2d-references.md`, "a slideshow
   with a character"). Four of A's eight beats are built around reading a poster or card: the four-panel emergence
   poster, the motto with its two cards, the zoom into panel G, and the end card. In B, nearly every idea is acted out
   by the theatre itself: ants spell answers, judges score all-or-nothing, a house rule caps the budget, casts swell
   while rehearsal stays flat. The paper-theatre look the user picked pays off most when the theatre is the metaphor,
   not only the stage for posters.
2. **B's comedy does the teaching.** The cat's per-digit grade (1/3, 2/3, 3/3) next to the judges' 0/0/100 *is*
   Schaeffer et al.'s argument. They used GPT-3-family arithmetic with token edit distance (checked, section 4), so
   the analogy is unusually exact. "Budget is roughly cast times rehearsal" is FLOPs ~ 6ND, and the viewer can work
   out the Chinchilla result before it is shown.
3. **B keeps her emphasis.** She spends about 5 minutes on slide 9 and makes one point there: you cannot know what
   scale brings from small experiments ("you do not do the experiments, you will never know"). B says this outright
   ("Small models won't warn you"). A turns emergence into evidence for the cat's bet and puts the counterpoint on a
   sticky note.

A is the cleaner explainer: better hook, sharper single idea, fewer errors. Its best parts transfer to B at low cost:
the motto framing, the stamp on "resources*", the bet as the X post and poll, and the verified panel-G fact.

## 2. Scores, criterion by criterion

### Hook (first 3 s)
- **A.** Frame 0: "Same budget. / Who wins?", two felt puppets wearing "280B" and "70B" sashes, and the cat pushing a
  chip onto BIG at 0.4 s. On X it is a poll in one picture, and the post can attach a real X poll (bible hook #2 and
  #5). Insiders already know the answer (A says so, risk 8), but the bet still frames the whole film. On Douyin,
  "押大押小" is catchy, but it is dice-gambling vocabulary on a platform that moderates gambling content, and
  "2800亿 / 700亿" means nothing to a mass audience before the sashes are explained. Use the fallback
  "大的赢，还是小的赢？" or "同样的预算，押哪个？".
- **B.** Frame 0: "One ant / can't add.", a felt ant under a gel spot, judges at 0 0 0, the cat's "Rehearse more?".
  It is a good Douyin thumbnail (cute plus a small mystery, 8 characters), and there is motion before 1 s. On a muted
  X timeline nothing says AI or LLM until 0:14. The X post text ("...Then a 70B model beat a 280B one on the same
  budget") carries the topic, but the picture does not. Frame 0 is also crowded with six pieces of text: strip,
  marquee, marquee small print, title, brass plate, sticky note. The bible allows at most two must-reads per shot.

### Clarity of the ONE idea
- **A** (8): one line runs from "same budget, who wins?" to "spend it well beats spend it big" to "this course
  teaches efficiency". The weak link is the tape-measure beat (12.4 s). It teaches that each log tick is 100x, which
  is a good fact, but its VO never connects that to the idea (no "every tick costs 100x more, so waste is the
  expensive part"). A also never says *why* the small model wins. It shows money turning into stuffing or into books,
  but not the size x data trade. Its "double the model, double the data" rule goes only into the post.
- **B** (7): emergence plus the honest limit (30 s), then Chinchilla (25 s), the bill (13 s), the motto and the course
  (12 s). Each section feeds the next ("bigger scored higher" leads to "casts grew" leads to "same budget, smaller won"
  leads to "bills went industrial, so waste is costly" leads to "efficiency x resources"). Still, it is a tour of three
  slides, and the motto comes last instead of being earned on screen.

### Comedy (four tests)
- **A** (7). The best moments: chips added on each piece of supporting evidence, then the chip tower collapses and the
  final frame moves the chip to SMALL (a running gag tied to the reveal). The tape measure, 1 mm to 1 km and out the
  door, passes the Resolution test because you only laugh if you get the log axis. The cat covering the counterpoint
  with a sticky note is confirmation bias, with the cat as the butt. Weaker: the stage floor bending under the $500B
  sign is decorative. The panel-G callback is clever but works mainly for experts.
- **B** (8.5). Strongest: the ant triple ("Zero. Still zero. ...it adds."); the cat's 1/3 to 3/3 slope next to the
  judges' 0/0/100 step (the Mirage paper, acted out); "Rehearsal: three hundred... three hundred... two-seventy" (a
  real number as the punchline); "Rehearsal's for small troupes" (the narrator playing the fool and voicing slide 11's
  wrong reading); and the sticky-note running gag "Rehearse more?" -> "REHEARSAL??" -> "Rehearse more.". Decorative:
  the desk lamp dimming and the giant-theatre pull-back. B admits this in its risk 9; see fix 7.

### Bilingual craft
- **A.** "大就完了？" (both "bigger and done" and "bigger and doomed") is a native ZH title with a separate EN title.
  "这个故事告诉我们" and "Moral:" each fit their language. "大，花钱能买；省，只能自己造" and "Size, you can buy.
  Efficiency, you have to build." are written separately. Faults: the ZH VO calls the models 地鼠 / 龙猫 as if those
  were their names, and the gambling phrase.
- **B.** "加量又加价" (a twist on "加量不加价") against a dry EN "Budgets went industrial."; "突然，变成了渐渐"; and
  "算力嘛……另计" with a held pause all land natively. Faults: "物理学家说：多者异也" is literary for a mass Douyin VO,
  and the Douyin title "大模型为什么越大越开窍" is anthropomorphic and promises the opposite of the payoff.

### Buildability in the paper-theatre + riso look, 9:16
- **A** (6). The theatre, the riso posters and the rod puppets with no joints are cheap. The cost is in B3: a crane
  down past the desk legs, painted room and night-street plates (three layers each), two whip pans, and a code-made
  felt world sharing the screen with painted plates. That breaks the bible's "写实与手绘同框" rule unless it is graded
  very carefully. It also needs three characters (the cat, a gopher, a chinchilla that must not look like Totoro), and
  the zoom into panel G has to be redrawn as vectors.
- **B** (6.5). It stays in one theatre and has two characters (the cat and the ants). It already contains
  ready-to-paste Codex / ChatGPT prompts for the felt cat and the felt ant (CLAUDE.md asks for these). The real costs:
  hundreds to thousands of instanced ants with per-plane depth of field and per-ant card pixels that have to spell
  digits you can read at phone size, the two-tier stage in B5, and the 30x pull-back in B6, which is the most
  expensive shot and decorative. Safe zones are respected in both scripts. B's bill board spans x 60-1020 only above
  y 700, which is allowed.

### Fit with the lecture
Her weighting (from the recording): slide 9 gets about 4 min 50 s (her career, the ChatGPT surprise, scaling research
as a chicken-and-egg money problem, flat-then-sudden curves, Anderson, the grow-it-or-compress-it research cycle).
Slide 10 gets about 1 min 55 s and is about cost, not the four numbers. Slide 11 gets about 40 s and makes one claim
("a lot of research are indeed driven by this kind of efficiency concerns ... that's why we want to develop this
class"). She never says "Chinchilla", "70B vs 175B" or the motto aloud.
- **A** (6): the spine (the bet, then Chinchilla) is slide 11's last bullet. Emergence is shrunk to a supporting
  poster, and "small models can't tell you" is missing. The bills and the course ending are kept.
- **B** (7.5): slide 9 as she teaches it (flat, then suddenly, and you cannot know from small models), Anderson as
  "properties of the whole", the counterpoint the slide cites, the bills, and efficiency as the reason for the course.
- **Neither** uses her strongest material: scaling research as a money problem, and the research cycle ("If you can
  make it larger, then the idea is how can you efficiently make it larger?"). That cycle line *is* this episode's ONE
  idea in her own words. See section 5.

   cat's stamp "*compute not included" / "*算力另计" goes on that asterisk. That makes the brand line self-explaining:
