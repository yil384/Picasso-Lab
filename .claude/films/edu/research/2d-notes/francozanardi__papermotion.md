## Stack
TypeScript (strict) + Vite + Canvas2D, Node 22.18+ running .ts natively, playwright-core driving Chromium headless,
ffmpeg. Vitest tests (11 files: physics, film, fire, snow, Beats, timeline, audio ...). Engine modules: `core` (vectors,
easing, IK, seeded noise, springs), `physics` (deterministic Verlet World, one-way Surfaces, Plate with aerofoil drag,
rope, SoftBody, Roller), `rig` (spring-driven Skeleton, Gait for bipeds/quadrupeds, Strand, Hair), `motion` (Swimmer,
steer, School boids, Leap), `paper` (the renderer), `scenery` (ridges, parametric flora, sward lawns, buildings, star
sky), `weather` (rain, snow), `fx` (particles, Tracks, Fire), `camera` (spring-follow, parallax layers by depth),
`direction` (Beats state machine, Edit shot list), `audio` (SoundLog cues, parametric voices and instruments, Mixer),
`stage` (fixed-timestep Stage with pre-roll, probes, grade/grain/letterbox, transitions).

## Pipeline
- Unlike the p5 kits, frames are NOT pure functions of t: a fixed-timestep deterministic simulation runs forward
  (0.6 s pre-roll so hair/ropes settle), frames must be requested in increasing order, seeded rng only.
- `Paper` draws each polygon as cut paper: a torn edge that boils every few frames (stable per seed), fibre texture,
  drop shadow, a light "cut core" edge, rim light on the side facing `paper.light` and a core-shadow band on the far
  side. `paper.sheet` merges a whole character into ONE piece of paper so limbs don't read as stickers.
- Sound is generated from simulation events: `this.cue('step', {gain, pan})` where a foot lands, `this.level('roll',
  speed)`; `soundtrack(sampleRate)` builds voices + score + Mixer into a WAV; render muxes it.
- `pnpm render <name>` pipes every frame to ffmpeg (1280x720 30 fps in the gallery); `pnpm grab <name> t... [--probe]`
  writes frames + a probe dump (positions, current beat, beat history); `pnpm sheet`; `pnpm listen` (wav,
  spectrogram, loudness, cue list as the agent's "ears").
- Measured here: `pnpm install` 80 MB; `CHROMIUM_PATH=<Chrome> pnpm grab rooftops 2.5 6` rendered both 1920x1080 frames
  (the contact-sheet step then failed only because this ffmpeg build lacks the `drawtext` filter).

## How Claude is guided
- `.claude/skills/papermotion/SKILL.md` (+ an `.agents/` copy for other agents, slightly older) with references:
  engine-api, scenes, characters, art-direction, inspection, sound, pitfalls; `docs/field-notes.md` (374 lines) and
  `AGENTS.md`.
- Principles: "Generated by code. No image files, no image models"; "Engine vs content" (new reusable behaviour goes in
  `src/` as a parameterised primitive with a test; story content stays in the scene; "Don't grow a catalog of props");
  "Original work" (AGENTS.md: "Making a new film here: don't open the other `examples/`" because agents reuse cast and
  beats); "Life at bone level" (motion from intents driving springs/gaits/physics, "never from keyframing positions");
  "Acceptable within the universe" (physics reads right for paper, not realistic); "Judge motion only from the
  rendered MP4".
- Workflow: pitch (10-30 s, beginning/turn/ending, "Prefer one clear action over subtle symbols: viewers didn't read a
  lost shoe or an unravelling scarf as metaphors"), beats per actor, shots, environment interactions; build in passes
  (blocking -> motion -> look -> detail pass -> finishing -> sound); probes before frames; report honestly and append to
  field notes.
- A concrete quality-bar checklist ("Each character is one piece of paper", "Nothing near the camera is a flat color:
  core shadow, rim light and at least two tones", "Eyes have life", "One light direction per shot", "Layers separate
  by value: far layers take on the color of the air", "Cuts happen on action", "Close-ups keep line weights thin").
- pitfalls.md is a symptom -> cause -> fix table (e.g. "A character looks like a pile of parts" -> "One `paper.sheet`
  per character"; "A cut shows the wrong moment" -> "Trigger shots from beats").
- Human input per film: "Most films were close to one-shot: the agent got a one-line brief and full creative freedom".

## What it looks like (frames viewed)
Downloaded all six gallery MP4s (1280x720, 30 fps; 10-30 s) via the README's signed attachment URLs and viewed frame
sheets: `frames/papermotion/sheet_snow.jpg`, `sheet_demo.jpg`, `sheet_rooftops.jpg`, `sheet_sea.jpg`,
`strip_rooftops_leap.jpg` (12 frames across the cat's leap), `snow_t14.jpg`, my renders `render_rooftops_2.5.jpg` /
`render_rooftops_6.jpg` (1920x1080), and for comparison the GPT-labelled `sheet_gpt6astra_light_embers.jpg`.
- Look: clean cut-paper illustration with soft gradients, torn light-edged silhouettes, paper fibre visible at full
  res, glows for moon/lamps, letterbox and grain on some films. Parallax layers with aerial perspective (snow village,
  sunset meadow, underwater light shafts, moonlit skyline with lit windows).
- Best: Tejados. A tuxedo cat with a convincing quadruped gait, tail strand and crouch-before-leap, a pigeon that
  startles, smooth spring-follow camera. Marea: moon jelly with soft-body bell and trailing tentacle strands, a
  fish school (boids) through kelp and light shafts. First snow: Clawd rolls a snowball that physically grows, rolls
  down the hill and becomes a snowman with a tree-branch arm; snowfall and footprints.
- Weak: Clawd is a stiff block with tiny dot eyes and tiny legs, often small in frame; the meadow and snow sets look
  like a tidy mobile-game background (pleasant, a bit generic); far skylines can read as thin line art; little acting
  or comedy. The texture is subtle compared with bohemian-tokenry's felt.

## Quality
6.5/10 overall (Tejados and Marea 7.5; the Clawd shorts 6). Strongest on physically grounded secondary motion
(strands, soft bodies, gaits, weather) and sound-from-events; weakest on character appeal and staging for comedy.

## What Picasso Lab should borrow
1. Motion via a deterministic simulation for secondary motion (hair/cable strands, ropes that sag, soft-body blobs,
   particle weather) layered under hand-keyed acting. For LLM-systems explainers: a token stream as a rope, a KV cache
   as soft bodies stacking, a scheduler as boids.
2. The "one piece of paper per character" rule and the material model: torn edge that boils per seed, fibre texture,
   drop shadow, rim light from ONE scene light direction, a core-shadow band. Our 3D renders can supply the light
   direction and depth, the 2D layer stays paper.
3. Sound designed from simulation events (cue log -> parametric voices -> mixer) plus a `listen` tool with
   spectrogram and loudness numbers as the agent's ears.
4. Probes: a `probe()` per scene dumping positions, current beat and beat history, so timing is checked as numbers
   before frames.
5. Beats as a state machine with condition-triggered transitions and shots triggered by beats, not by the clock.
6. Engine-vs-content discipline with a test per new primitive, and the template trick of shipping lessons (skill +
   field notes + pitfalls table) but not prior films, so each new episode stays original.
7. The art-direction table of parallax depths (backdrop 0, sky 0.02-0.06, hills 0.1-0.3, mid 0.35-0.6, action 1,
   foreground 1.2-1.5 blurred) as a default layer plan for 9:16 sets.
