Objectives

  • Understand major systems bottlenecks in LLM training and inference (compute, memory, latency, etc.).
  • Gain a broad view of key research topics and trends in LLM efficiency, and be able to read and critically evaluate recent papers.
  • Understand how state-of-the-art techniques evolved over time and the trade-offs behind them.

Structure (tentative): foundation models (~3 lectures) + training (~4 lectures) + project preparation and presentations.

Prerequisites

This class is an interdisciplinary class covering many up-to-date topics and developments in machine learning, data, and systems. If you have never taken any of the following classes (or equivalent), you might need to allocate more time to prepare yourself.

  • MATH 18 (Linear Algebra) or equivalent
  • CSE 151B (Deep Learning) or equivalent
  • CSE 132C or CSE 120 (Operating Systems) or equivalent
  • DSC 102 (Systems for Scalable Analytics) or equivalent; or substantial practical experience with scalable data systems and ML algorithms
  • Proficiency in Python programming
  • Knowledge of deep learning and frameworks such as Tensor, PyTorch, HuggingFace
  • Highly preferred (not required): DSC 204A (Scalable Data Analytics), DSC 240 (Machine Learning)

Requirements and prerequisites

What are the prerequisites for this course?

You need to have completed either:

  • A course on ML algorithms (e.g., CSE 151) is absolutely necessary.
  • A course on either database systems internals (e.g., CSE 132C) or operating systems (e.g., CSE 120) is also necessary.
  • DSC 102 suffices as a prerequisite for both of the above aspects.
  • Substantial project or industrial experience on relevant topics can be substituted for prior coursework, subject to the instructor's consent. Email the instructor if you would like to enroll but are unsure if you satisfy the prerequisites.
I have relevant industry experience but haven't taken the prerequisite courses. Can I still enroll?

Yes, substantial project or industrial experience can be considered as a substitute for prerequisites. Email the instructor directly to discuss your background and eligibility.

Content and structure

What is the main focus of this course?

This is a research-based course that explores systems for machine learning, combining elements of ML/AI and systems. In this year, we will focus more on LLMs, its related system support, optimizations, deployment challenges, and emerging problems.

Who is this course designed for?

The course is primarily tailored for MS students, PhD students, and advanced undergraduate students who are interested in systems for scalable data science and ML engineering.

What are the suggested readings for this course?

Suggested textbooks:

  • Recommended: Data Management in Machine Learning Systems, by Matthias Boehm, Arun Kumar, and Jun Yang (free ebook via UCSD VPN).
  • Additional (optional) for background/foundations: Machine Learning, by Tom Mitchell (McGraw Hill); Deep Learning, by Ian Goodfellow, Yoshua Bengio, and Aaron Courville (MIT Press); Operating Systems: Three Easy Pieces, by Remzi and Andrea Arpaci-Dusseau (free ebook); mlsysbook.ai.
