## What I see
- Home: warm paper page framed by a painted watercolor network (sage/teal crowns of nodes and trembling ink branches down the margins, layered blue-grey mountain sierras at the bottom). It is generated by numpy (`home/acuarela/paint.py`, from the author's private `acuarela` watercolor library: "paper, washes, trembling ink, sierras"); the mountains are literally kernel-density estimates of the network's node x-positions. The page "paints" a river as you scroll.
- Explorable: dark hero card with a perspective floor of squares, a grid of real gelu-4l activation tiles in blue, an eye icon (the monitor) and a big step counter; then a serif long-read on paper with figures re-plotted "on paper, in ink colors", each with a badge (SCHEMATIC / REAL / SPECULATIVE).

## Concept / narration / pacing
- Text + interaction, no VO. Pacing doctrine (kit/PLAYBOOK.md "Timing: write the reads", adapted from ClaudeAnimationBase): "A read is one thing the viewer has to understand ... Write them down for every animated step or video beat, in order, with times ... One read at a time, and the cause before its effect ... Fast actions, slow meanings ... Lead the eye before an important read". Camera rule: "one camera move, motivated by the story ... the content must already be there when the shot opens."
- Process: brief -> sources actually read -> claims ledger (sourced/simplified/speculative) -> script -> technical gate by a different model -> hardest visual first -> contact-sheet review -> adversarial fact-check -> simulated learner.

## Good / slide-like
- As a video reference it is weak (web page, the format the user rejects). The procedural watercolor frame is the one genuinely crafted visual, and its data-driven "mountains are a density plot" idea is a charming kind of honesty.
- The "reads" timing rule and the cross-model review loop are the transferable parts.
